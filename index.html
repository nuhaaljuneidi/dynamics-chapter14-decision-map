Newton's Second Law — §14.1
ΣF = ma
One particle, or a system of particles?
System of Particles — §14.1
ΣF = Σmᵢaᵢ = maG
m = Σmᵢ  (G = mass center)
Details
Newton's Third Law: internal action–reaction forces between particles in the system cancel in pairs — only external forces contribute to ΣF.
Treat connected masses (coupled cars, blocks in contact) as one effective particle at G, or isolate each mass with its own free-body and kinetic diagram.
One Particle
ΣF = ma for a single particle
Details
Newton's First Law: if ΣF = 0, the particle is in equilibrium (a = 0) — a static or constant-velocity case.
Which coordinate system fits the motion?
Free-body diagram = kinetic diagram, resolved the same way
Cartesian Coordinates — §14.2
ΣFx = max
ΣFy = ma_y
ΣFz = maz
Details
Simplest when forces and acceleration are naturally described along fixed x,y,z axes — including straight-line (1-D) motion.
Procedure:
1. Free-body diagram (all forces).
2. Kinetic diagram (ma, same components).
3. Solve ΣF = ma component by component.
Normal & Tangential Components — §14.3
ΣFt = mat
ΣFn = man = mv²/ρ
Details
Useful when the path's curvature is known and you're tracking speed change (a_t) separately from direction change (a_n).
a_n always points toward the concave side of the path (center of curvature).
Polar & Cylindrical Coordinates — §14.4
ΣFr = mar = m(r̈−rω²)
ΣFθ = maθ = m(rα+2ṙω)
ΣFz = maz
Details
Useful when position or constraints are naturally expressed with radial distance r and angle θ — a rod, arm, or slot problem.
Orbital Mechanics — §14.5
ΣFr = −GMm/r²  (gravity is the only radial force)
Same polar equations of motion; the central force being purely radial (ΣFθ = 0) is what produces a conic-section orbit.
Choose the coordinate system from what the problem hands you — not from habit.
• Cartesian: forces/acceleration already along fixed axes, or a straight-line problem.
• Normal–tangential: you know the path's curvature and care about speed vs. direction.
• Polar/cylindrical: a rod, arm, slot, or a central (gravity-like) force defines the motion.
